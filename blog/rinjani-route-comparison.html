Sembalun, Senaru or Torean?
How to Choose Your Rinjani Trekking Route

Written by the Rinjani Trekking Pro guides, on the mountain since 2016 · 9 min read · Updated November 2026

Most route guides compare Rinjani's trails as if every hiker walks at the same speed. After guiding on this mountain since 2016, we can tell you that is not how it works. The right route for your Rinjani trekking depends less on the mountain and more on you: how fast you walk, how often you hike at home, and how many days you have in Lombok.

Mount Rinjani (3,726 m) has three main official routes: Sembalun, Senaru and Torean. Below is our honest comparison, including the parts other websites leave out, like how long the summit really takes and the most common mistake we see on the trail.

The short answer

  • →You are fit, hike often and have only 2 days: go up via Sembalun for the summit (2D1N Summit).
  • →It is your first big mountain, or you prefer a steady pace: take 3 or 4 days. The extra night makes the summit much more enjoyable (3D2N or 4D3N).
  • →You love forest, shade and want to avoid the heat: start from Senaru.
  • →You want summit, lake, hot springs and a wild canyon on the way down: the Sembalun to Torean traverse (3D2N Sembalun–Torean).

1 Sembalun Route: the way to the summit

Sembalun is on the east side of the mountain and is the route almost every summit trek starts from. You walk through open savanna with big views all day, up to the Sembalun crater rim camp, where you sleep before the summit push.

The hard parts

  • Bukit Penyesalan ("the Hill of Regret"): a long series of steep, rocky hills before the crater rim. This is where most hikers first feel the mountain.
  • The summit push: the last part to the top is loose volcanic sand and gravel. You take two steps up and slide back one. It is slow for everyone, so do not worry if you feel slow here.
  • Sun and heat: the savanna has very little shade, so the middle of the day can be hot.

Water

There are springs on the mountain, but in the dry season they become very small and only give a little water, much less than in the rainy season. This is one of the reasons we always plan drinking water for the whole group in advance.

How long does the summit really take?

This is the question we get most, and the honest answer is: it depends a lot on your pace. From the Sembalun crater rim camp to the summit, the difference between a fast group and a slower group is usually 3 to 4 hours.

Slower groups start earlier to give themselves a chance at sunrise, but honestly, many still arrive after the sun is up. That is completely fine. The view of the crater, Segara Anak lake and Bali is still spectacular at 8 or 9 in the morning.

2 Senaru Route: shade, forest and sunset

Senaru is on the north side, and it is our home village. The trail climbs through tropical rainforest up to the Senaru crater rim, with sunset views over Segara Anak lake and Mount Agung in Bali.

Good

  • Shady forest, so it is not hot
  • More water along the trail than on Sembalun
  • Quieter, with beautiful sunsets from the crater rim

Less good

  • A long way, with steady uphill all day
  • Roots and rocks, slippery after rain
  • Not the route for a quick summit

⏱️ Packages from Senaru: 2D1N Senaru Crater Rim, 2D1N Stampol, and the way down on 3D2N and 4D3N treks.

3 Torean Route: the canyon way down

Torean is used as the way down from Segara Anak lake. The trail follows a deep canyon with waterfalls, rivers and tall cliffs. From the lake to the end of the trail in Torean village takes about 6 hours.

Good

  • The most dramatic scenery on Rinjani
  • Hot springs near the lake at the start
  • Quiet and wild

Less good

  • Narrow paths along cliffs
  • Slippery when wet
  • A long day for tired legs after the summit

⏱️ Package: 3D2N Sembalun–Torean (summit, lake, hot springs, canyon).

The #1 mistake we see on every route

It is not choosing the wrong trail. It is walking too fast at the start. Hikers are excited, they push hard in the first hours, then stop often and sit down straight away. That is exactly when leg cramps start, and cramps are the most common reason people struggle on the summit day.

Our guides' tip: slow but steady always wins.

  • Walk at a pace where you can still talk.
  • Take short breaks standing up instead of many long sit-downs.
  • On a mountain, especially your first one, it is not about how fast you walk. It is about walking without stopping too much.

Weather and wind

Expect it to be cold at night, around 15°C, and colder when the wind blows. The weather on Rinjani can change quickly, so pack for sun, rain and cold on the same trip. August, September and October are the windiest months: bring a proper windproof jacket, a beanie and gloves for the summit night. See our Rinjani packing list for the full list.

Questions we often get

Which route is the easiest on Rinjani?

No route is easy, and how hard it feels depends mostly on your pace. If you have never climbed a big mountain, the Senaru crater rim (2 days) or a 3 to 4 day summit trek with an extra night is the most comfortable choice.

Can I see the sunrise from the summit?

Fit hikers who start around 3:00 AM usually reach the summit at about 6:30 AM, in time for sunrise. Slower hikers start at 1:00 to 2:00 AM and may arrive between 7:00 and 9:00 AM.

I am over 50 and not very fast. Can I still reach the summit?

Yes. Many of our guests are older hikers. We walk at your pace and our guides will help you reach the summit, even if it is later in the morning. Choose a 3 or 4 day trek so you are not rushed.

What is the difference between a sharing group and trekking only?

Both are sharing group treks with the same guides, porters, tents and meals. The sharing group tour also includes pick-up in Lombok and a homestay night in Senaru. With trekking only, you meet our team at the start of the trek in Senaru or Sembalun. See all prices.
